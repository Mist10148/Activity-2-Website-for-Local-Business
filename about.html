ABOUT US

Our Story

[BUSINESS NAME] started the way most small Iloilo shops do: with one second-hand espresso machine, a folding table, and a family that could not stop arguing about how dark a roast should really be. The first customers were neighbors who came for the smell before they ever came for the coffee.

What began as a weekend stall in [BARANGAY] slowly turned into a proper shop. The owners spent a year visiting farms around Panay, tasting whatever the growers were willing to share, until they settled on the two blends we still pour today. Nothing about that process was fast, and that is rather the point.

Today the shop seats about thirty people, and on most afternoons every one of those seats is taken by a student with a laptop, a tricycle driver on break, or someone who simply wanted an hour to themselves. We never rush anyone out. A cup bought here comes with the table it is sitting on.

The menu has grown along with the regulars. Pastries came first, because customers kept asking for something to go with their cortado. Rice meals followed once people started staying through lunch. Every item on the board arrived because somebody asked for it, which is why none of it feels borrowed from another cafe.

We are still a small business, and that is not an apology. Being small is what lets us know our suppliers by name, change the single-origin whenever a better harvest comes in, and remember how you take your coffee by your third visit. That is the whole plan, and we intend to stick to it.

Mission, Vision and Values

Mission

To serve honest, carefully made coffee at a price an Ilonggo student or worker can actually afford, and to give the neighborhood a comfortable place to sit while they drink it.

Vision

To become the coffee shop Iloilo thinks of first when it wants something local rather than imported, and to keep every bean we pour sourced from farms on our own island.

Values

  • Local first — our beans, our bakers and our staff all come from Iloilo.
  • Made slowly — nothing leaves the counter before it is right.
  • Fair prices — good coffee should not be a luxury item.
  • Room to stay — a table here is yours for as long as you need it.
  • Honest waste — unsold pastries go to the neighbors, never to the bin.
